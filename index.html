AgentSearch

Constructing and Evaluating Agentic Solutions in Open Ecosystems

ICLR 2027 · San Francisco, USA · April 29 or 30, 2027 (TBD)

The Second AgentSearch Workshop · Proposal under review

Call for Papers 1st Edition @ SIGIR 2026

About

AI agents are changing how people access digital services and accomplish tasks. In open ecosystems, agents, tools, and skills from independent providers can be discovered and combined across provider boundaries. For both users seeking help with a task and orchestration systems acting on their behalf, the central question is which agentic solution to use or how to assemble one. Construction is difficult because components are heterogeneous, their descriptions may not reflect their capabilities for a given task, independent updates can invalidate earlier choices, and provider incentives may not align with users' objectives. Execution brings further challenges as solutions use task data and context and coordinate actions across providers, raising concerns about reliability, trust, and security.

This workshop examines how agentic solutions can be discovered, composed, evaluated, and adapted under these conditions. It brings together research on agent and tool discovery, routing, orchestration, execution-grounded evaluation, and diagnosis, while incorporating economic perspectives on incentives and strategic interactions and security perspectives on trustworthy component selection and cross-provider execution. Building on the AgentSearch workshop at SIGIR 2026, it extends the focus from retrieving and ranking components to constructing and assessing agentic solutions in open ecosystems.

Topics of Interest

Agent & Tool Discovery, Selection, and Composition

  • Representation, profiling, and indexing of agent, tool, model, and skill capabilities
  • Agent and tool retrieval, ranking, recommendation, and search
  • Task-, constraint-, cost-, and reliability-aware component selection
  • Model and agent routing, tool selection, and skill selection
  • Multi-agent team formation, composition, and orchestration
  • Agentic workflow construction and harness configuration
  • Compatibility and collective suitability of heterogeneous components

Evaluation, Diagnosis, and Reliability of Agentic Solutions

  • Execution-grounded evaluation of agents, tools, skills, and composed systems
  • Benchmarks, datasets, and evaluation protocols for agentic solutions
  • Task success, efficiency, reliability, and robustness metrics
  • Evaluation of multi-agent collaboration and component interactions
  • Credit assignment, failure attribution, and diagnosis
  • Trajectory-level and process-level evaluation
  • Evaluation under distribution shifts and changing environments
  • Trustworthiness and human-centered evaluation

Learning and Adaptation in Open Agent Ecosystems

  • Learning from execution feedback and interaction histories
  • Adaptive agent and tool routing, selection, and orchestration
  • Dynamic component replacement and workflow reconfiguration
  • Continual adaptation to evolving agent capabilities and availability
  • Optimization of agentic configurations and workflows
  • Shared memory, experience reuse, and knowledge transfer across agents
Economic Lens

Economics of Agentic Systems and Market Mechanisms

  • Agentic marketplaces and market-based coordination
  • Auctions, bidding, and pricing mechanisms for agent and task allocation
  • Mechanism and incentive design for agent routing, ranking, cooperation, and resource sharing
  • Compensation, attribution, and rewards for contributed agents, tools, skills, and artifacts
  • Strategic behavior, competition, and collusion among agents
  • Agent self-assessment of capabilities, costs, and resource requirements
  • Automated and learning-based mechanism design
  • Marketplace simulation and evaluation of welfare, fairness, and market dynamics
Security Lens

Security and Trust in Open Agent Ecosystems

  • Security of agent, tool, and skill discovery and selection
  • Supply-chain attacks on agent registries and third-party components
  • Adversarial manipulation of agent descriptions, rankings, and recommendations
  • Prompt injection and attacks propagating across agents
  • Poisoning of shared memory, trajectories, skills, and other artifacts
  • Coordinated attacks, covert communication, and collusion among agents
  • Provenance, verification, and access control for third-party components
  • Secure agent composition, isolation, and permission management
  • Red-teaming, threat modeling, and security benchmarks for agent ecosystems

Call for Papers

The second AgentSearch workshop invites submissions describing original research findings, preliminary results, ongoing research, new methodologies, benchmarks, systems, and perspectives related to constructing and evaluating agentic solutions in open ecosystems. We particularly encourage work in progress and emerging research directions that can benefit from feedback and discussion within the workshop community.

Submission Types

  • Tiny / Short Papers (2–4 pages): preliminary findings, late-breaking results, focused empirical analyses, novel ideas, follow-up experiments, or perspectives on emerging research problems.
  • Research Papers (up to 8 pages): original research on methods, models, algorithms, theoretical foundations, empirical studies, or systems.
  • Perspective and Position Papers (4–8 pages): important open problems, new research directions, challenges to existing assumptions, or conceptual frameworks.
  • Benchmark, Dataset, and Demo Papers (4–8 pages): evaluation benchmarks, datasets, shared resources, software frameworks, or demonstrations of agentic systems and infrastructure.

Page limits exclude references.

Submission Instructions

All submissions must be written in English and submitted as PDF files through OpenReview, following the official ICLR 2027 formatting guidelines. The submission template and OpenReview link will be announced on this website. All submissions undergo double-blind peer review and are evaluated on technical quality, originality, clarity, relevance to the workshop, and potential to stimulate discussion. At least one author of each accepted submission is expected to register for the workshop and present the work.

Non-Archival Policy

The workshop is non-archival. Accepted submissions will be made publicly available through OpenReview and linked from the workshop website, but will not constitute archival publications. Authors may further develop their work and submit it to archival venues, subject to the policies of those venues.

Previous Edition: AgentSearch @ SIGIR 2026

The first AgentSearch workshop, The First Workshop on Indexing, Retrieval, and Ranking of AI Agents, was held at SIGIR 2026 in Melbourne | Naarm, Australia, on July 24, 2026. It framed agent search as a first-class information retrieval problem and focused on the discovery, retrieval, and ranking of individual agents and tools. The ICLR 2027 edition broadens this scope to constructing and evaluating complete agentic solutions in open ecosystems.

Highlights

  • Accepted Papers: 11 workshop papers on agent retrieval, routing, evaluation, and governance
  • Program: keynotes, invited talks, oral and poster presentations, and a panel discussion
  • AgentSearch Challenge: retrieval and reranking over nearly 10,000 real-world agents, with labels derived from execution traces
